### Debate Summary

Both analysts delivered rigorous, data-driven arguments. The bull built a compelling long‑term transformation case: record free cash flow, aggressive debt retirement, a shrinking negative equity overhang, and a secular uptrend supported by the golden cross. The bear countered with a sharp short‑term lens: deteriorating momentum, quality issues in the Q1 numbers (margin compression, working‑capital driven FCF), premium valuation vs. peers, and rising macro risk for a high‑beta name.  

The core tension is *timeframe*. The bull is an investor looking through the pullback; the bear is a trader reacting to weakening technicals and cracks in the most recent quarter. Neither side landed a knockout blow, but the bear raised concrete near‑term risks that the bull could not fully dismiss—particularly the 7% sequential decline in operating margin and the $500M surge in accounts payable that flattered free cash flow.

Because the bull’s long‑term thesis remains intact but the short‑term risk/reward is not clearly skewed, this is one of those rare instances where **Hold is the appropriate verdict**. The evidence is genuinely balanced: forcing a Buy or a Sell here would be premature.
