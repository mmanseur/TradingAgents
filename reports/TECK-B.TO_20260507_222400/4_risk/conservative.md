## The FCF Problem Nobody Wants to Discuss

The Aggressive Analyst's treatment of negative free cash flow is actually my biggest concern here, and I want to be surgical about why this matters.

They claim: "CapEx is cyclical. You front-load it during development phases, then harvest cash during production ramp. TECK is at that inflection point."

Okay. Let's test this claim against actual financial reality. The company reported:
- Operating cash flow (2025): $1.479 billion
- CapEx (2025): $2.062 billion
- Free cash flow: **-$583 million**
- Meanwhile, they paid out: $1.257 billion in buybacks and dividends

So TECK is **simultaneously running negative free cash flow AND returning capital to shareholders**. The Aggressive Analyst frames this as "management has options" and "they chose to reduce CapEx." But reducing from $2.635B to $2.062B—a 22% cut—still leaves them at a 139% CapEx-to-OCF ratio. That's not an inflection point; that's a structural problem.

Here's the uncomfortable truth: **If copper prices decline from current spot of $5.18 to $4.00 per pound, TECK's operating cash flow doesn't decline by 10%. It declines by 30-40%**, because of operating leverage in mining. A $1.18 price decline on maybe 200+ thousand tonnes of annual production isn't linear—it cascades through the P&L. Suddenly, that negative FCF becomes catastrophically negative.

The Aggressive Analyst says "assume copper stays at $4.50+, which is now the base case." But **that's exactly backward from risk management.** Your base case in planning should be conservative. Your valuation should assume *normalization*, not perpetuation of record prices. Copper averaged $3.50-4.00 from 2015-2020. The current $5.18 is an outlier, not a new floor. 

Even if the structural story is 100% correct, you don't value a cyclical commodity producer on peak prices. You value it on normalized prices. And at normalized copper prices of $3.75-4.00, TECK's earnings power contracts materially. The 22x P/E isn't "reasonable for a supercycle story"—it's *dangerous* because it prices in extended premium commodity pricing that may not persist.
