**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: This was a genuinely high-quality debate, and both sides made substantive points. Let me walk through where each side landed and why I'm tilting toward the bear—but not as aggressively as they'd like.

**Where the bull is right:**
The structural copper demand thesis has real legs. EVs, data centers, renewables, and grid modernization are not going away, and copper intensity per unit of economic activity is genuinely rising. That's not hype—it's physics and engineering. The bull is also correct that negative FCF during a capital-intensive expansion phase isn't automatically a death sentence for a miner; the industry context matters. And the technical trend is undeniably up—10 EMA > 50 SMA > 200 SMA is a healthy configuration. The bull's point about opportunity cost of waiting for a perfect entry is fair behavioral finance.

**Where the bull overreaches:**
The bull's CapEx normalization thesis is the weakest link. They claim CapEx drops to $1.5B and FCF magically turns positive, but the 5-year average is $2.34B, and there's no concrete evidence from management guidance that maintenance CapEx alone drops below $1.2-1.3B. Growth CapEx shifts, it doesn't disappear—Kay Copper will demand heavy spending in 2028-2031. The bull's demand projections (35M EVs by 2030, 20M data center racks) are materially above consensus forecasts like IEA's. And calling a 22x P/E "a bargain" for a cyclical commodity producer requires very aggressive assumptions about where we are in the cycle.

**Where the bear is right—and this is what tips the scales:**
1. **Cash burn is real and quantifiable.** Cash dropped from $7.6B to $5.0B in one year (-$2.6B). At this rate, even with stable operations, the runway is 2-3 years before painful choices must be made. The bear's cash flow cascade math checks out.
2. **Valuation is stretched relative to peers.** Freeport at 12x, Rio at 9x, BHP at 12x—Teck at 22x requires a lot to go right. The forward P/E of ~20.5x assumes $4.05 EPS which itself assumes copper stays above $4.00/lb. If copper mean-reverts even modestly to $3.70-3.80 (still historically strong), EPS compresses to $2.40-2.70 and you're looking at 31-35x trailing earnings.
3. **CapEx as % of OCF at 139% vs. peers at 55-68% is a genuine red flag.** This isn't just "expansion phase"—this is a company spending far more than it generates. 
4. **The MACD divergence is worth noting.** Price making new highs while momentum indicators roll over has historically preceded 5-10% corrections in Teck specifically.

**Where the bear overreaches:**
The $25-35 recession scenario with 65% probability assigned to the "unraveling" case is too aggressive. The bear's timeline for a dividend cut (Q1 2027) assumes no improvement in operating cash flow, no CapEx flexibility, and no commodity price support—all simultaneously. That's stacking worst cases. Also, dismissing the copper supercycle entirely ignores that supply-side constraints (8-10 year mine development timelines) create a genuine floor under prices that didn't exist in prior cycles. Copper at $3.00 requires a severe global recession.

**The deciding factor:**
The asymmetry of risk is what settles this. At $84, if the bull is right (copper stays strong, CapEx normalizes, earnings grow), upside is 15-25% to $95-105. If the bear is right on even the moderate case (copper softens to $3.70, multiples compress to 16-18x), downside is 30-45% to $48-60. That's roughly 2:1 downside-to-upside asymmetry. You don't need to believe the bear's catastrophic scenario—just the normalization scenario—to see that risk/reward is unfavorable at current levels.

I'm not calling this a Sell because the structural copper story has enough merit to prevent a full exit, and timing commodity shorts is notoriously difficult. But I am calling it Underweight because the valuation premium over peers isn't justified by the fundamentals, the cash burn trajectory is concerning, and the risk/reward is skewed negatively at $84.
